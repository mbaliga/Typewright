Why the reviewer is unhappy, in pictures

You drew letters. They were turned into a font by rasterizing your art and tracing the pixels back into outlines. That is the whole problem, and everything the reviewer listed follows from it.

1. A letter is made of points. Yours has far too many.

Every outline is a loop of points. A good outline uses the fewest points that describe the shape, and uses curved segments where the shape curves. Here is your T as it exists in the shipped font, every point drawn, next to the same T drawn properly.

A T is two rectangles. It needs eight points. The shipped one has 1,763 because a tracing program walked around your letter one pixel at a time and dropped a point at every step. The dots you see are not a design. They are footprints.

2. Straight lines cannot make a curve. Curves can.

Font outlines have two kinds of point: ones the line passes through, and ones that sit off to the side and pull the line into a curve. That second kind is what makes a round shape actually round.

Your font contains zero of the second kind. Not few. None. Every curve in it is a many-sided polygon pretending to be a curve. That is what the reviewer meant by "lots of precision, many nodes" and he was being polite about it.

3. What the four complaints actually mean

  1. "One source, a Regular UFO with TT curves." The source file that ships with a font is meant to be the file you drew in. Yours was reverse engineered out of the finished font, so it inherited the traced polygons. It is a photocopy of a photocopy.
  2. "Italic is simply slanted Regular." True. It is your Regular sheared 9 degrees by a script. A real italic is drawn, with different letterforms. Google Fonts will not accept a mechanical slant.
  3. "The diacritics may need work." Accents were positioned by copying bounding boxes instead of by anchors, which are the marked attachment points a font uses to place an accent over any letter. Your font has no anchors at all.
  4. "Some characters have interesting designs." Those are the letters that were never yours. When your drawings were incomplete, missing letters were constructed by formula. They do not match your hand.

4. What fixes it

Your Figma file is the real source. It always was. Nothing needs to be redrawn from scratch, it needs to be brought across without being destroyed on the way.

5. What I need from you

  1. Export the glyphs from Figma as SVG. Select the letters and use Export, format SVG. One file per letter or one sheet, either is fine.
  2. Outline your strokes first. If a letter is drawn as a line with thickness, select it and use Outline Stroke, so the shape becomes a filled outline. Fonts only understand filled shapes, not strokes.
  3. Flatten each letter into one shape. If a letter is several overlapping pieces, union them, so each letter is a single closed shape with its counters as holes.
  4. Keep every letter in the same size frame, with the baseline at the same height in each. That frame is the em box and it is what makes letters line up.
  5. Tell me which letters exist in your file, so I know which ones are genuinely missing and need drawing rather than formula.

From those SVGs I can build a real source: curves preserved as you drew them, anchors added for the accents, and the font compiled from that source instead of from a picture of it.
